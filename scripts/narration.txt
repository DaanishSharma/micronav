## card | micronav | open-source positioning, built from first principles | Daanish Sharma
Every map app has to answer one question before anything else: where am I? I wanted to know how that really works. So I learned sensor fusion from zero, and built micronav, an open-source positioning engine, in plain Python.

## card | Every sensor in your phone lies | GPS: jumps by metres, and drops out under flyovers | Gyroscope: 25° off after one minute | Accelerometer: 119 m off after one minute
Here's the problem. Every sensor in your phone lies. GPS jumps around by several metres, and drops out under flyovers and between buildings. The gyroscope measures turning, but a tiny bias makes it drift twenty-five degrees in a single minute. And if you integrate the accelerometer twice to get position, you're a hundred and nineteen metres off within a minute. No single sensor is good enough. The trick is to combine them, so that each one covers for the others' mistakes.

## drive
This is a real eleven-minute drive in Patiala, recorded on one iPhone with phyphox, which is open source too. Orange is raw GPS. Blue is micronav. Under the hood is a fifteen-state error-state Kalman filter. A hundred times a second, the gyroscope and accelerometer predict where the car went. Once a second, GPS corrects that prediction, and the filter learns the sensors' biases as it goes. It also knows that cars don't slide sideways, or take off. That one rule is surprisingly powerful. And because nobody mounts their phone perfectly straight, micronav works out how the phone sits in the car on its own, just from braking and turning.

## outage
Now the real test. What happens when GPS disappears? I hid it for thirty seconds, out of every ninety, and asked each filter to guess where the car was. A Kalman filter using GPS alone keeps going straight, and misses the hidden fixes by twenty-nine metres. micronav, fusing the motion sensors with what it knows about cars, misses by nine. That's three times better, with no extra hardware.

## matched
Finally, the track is snapped onto real roads from OpenStreetMap. Not just to the nearest road, which jumps between parallel streets, but with a hidden Markov model that finds the most likely route the car actually drove.

## card | What's inside | 1-D Kalman filter  ·  orientation from gyro, gravity and compass | GPS filter + RTS smoother  ·  HMM map matching | phone-in-car alignment  ·  15-state error-state EKF | roads as measurements  ·  batch least-squares smoothing
micronav is built chapter by chapter. A Kalman filter in one dimension. Orientation from the gyro, gravity and the compass. GPS smoothing. Map matching. Then the full fifteen-state filter, and the road itself as a measurement. Every formula is written out, and every filter is tested against a simulation where the true answer is known.

Next, I want a native phone recorder, support for NavIC, India's own satellite navigation, and more open map sources. The goal is simple: a positioning stack that anyone can read, run and trust.
